# Staying connected in Seoul

## Public Wi-Fi
Free public Wi-Fi is common in Seoul. You will find it in subway stations and on many
trains and buses, in cafes, shopping malls, and many public buildings. Networks named
with "Seoul" or "Public WiFi" are often free city networks. Public Wi-Fi is convenient
but not always reliable for navigation while moving around, so many visitors also get a
mobile data option.

## SIM cards and eSIM
Short-term prepaid tourist SIM cards are sold at the airport arrival halls, at some
convenience stores, and online for pickup. They give you a local number and/or data for
a set number of days. Many newer phones support eSIM, which you can buy and activate
online before you arrive, so you have data the moment you land. Compare data-only versus
voice-and-data plans based on whether you need to make local calls.

## Pocket Wi-Fi
A pocket Wi-Fi (portable router) gives several devices internet access at once and can be
rented at the airport or reserved online for airport pickup. It is useful for groups or
travelers with multiple devices, though it is one more thing to carry and charge.

## Which option to choose
If you travel solo and mainly need maps and messaging, an eSIM or data SIM is usually
simplest. For a group sharing one connection, pocket Wi-Fi can be cost-effective. Public
Wi-Fi alone is workable if you plan routes before heading out, but a mobile data option
makes getting around much easier.
