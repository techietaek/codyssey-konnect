# Understanding Seoul's culture

## Palaces (궁궐)
Seoul has several grand palaces from the Joseon dynasty, including Gyeongbokgung,
Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung, and Gyeonghuigung. They feature throne
halls, gardens, and gates, and some host changing-of-the-guard ceremonies. Changdeokgung's
"Secret Garden" (Huwon) is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Palaces are outdoor-heavy, so they
suit good weather. For opening days, ceremony times, and entry rules, check official information.

## Hanok (한옥)
A hanok is a traditional Korean house built with wood, tile roofs, and an underfloor heating
system called ondol. Bukchon Hanok Village and Ikseon-dong are well-known neighborhoods where
you can walk among hanok; some are now cafes, shops, and guesthouses. These are living
residential areas, so visitors are asked to keep noise down.

## Hanbok (한복)
Hanbok is traditional Korean clothing with simple lines and vivid colors. Rental shops near the
palaces let you wear hanbok for a few hours or a day, and wearing it often grants free or
discounted palace admission. It is a popular way to take photos in historic settings.

## Temples and Buddhism
Buddhist temples such as Jogyesa in central Seoul are active places of worship with halls,
pagodas, and lanterns. Some temples offer "templestay" programs where visitors experience
monastic life, meditation, and temple food. Dress modestly and be quiet inside halls.

## Jongmyo and Confucian heritage
Jongmyo Shrine is a UNESCO World Heritage site dedicated to the royal ancestral rites of the
Joseon kings; the Jongmyo Jerye ritual and its music are themselves recognized cultural heritage.
Confucian values shaped Korean etiquette around respect and hierarchy.

## Markets and modern culture
Traditional markets like Gwangjang and Namdaemun sell street food, textiles, and everyday goods,
while districts like Hongdae, Insadong, and Myeongdong mix galleries, craft shops, performance
streets, and shopping. Seoul blends centuries-old heritage with contemporary art, music, and design.

## Types of cultural experiences
When planning, it helps to think in categories: historic visits (palaces, shrines, hanok areas),
exhibitions (museums and galleries), live performances (traditional and modern), festivals and
seasonal events, and hands-on activities (crafts, cooking, hanbok). What is actually available
depends on the day, the hours, and the season, so confirmed availability always comes from the
official source rather than general descriptions like these.
