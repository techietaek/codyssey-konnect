# Cultural etiquette in Seoul

## Shoes indoors
Take off your shoes before entering homes, many traditional restaurants with floor
seating, temples' indoor halls, and hanok (traditional house) interiors. Look for a
shoe rack or a step up to a raised floor as a signal. Wearing clean socks is a good idea.

## Greetings and respect
A slight bow is a common, polite greeting. Koreans place value on showing respect to
elders: offer and receive objects (especially money, cards, or gifts) with both hands or
with your right hand supported by your left, and wait for elders to begin eating.

## Dining manners
Shared dishes are normal, and it is polite to pour drinks for others rather than yourself.
When someone older pours you a drink, hold your glass with both hands. Lifting rice or soup
bowls off the table is generally avoided; use the spoon for rice and soup and chopsticks for
side dishes. Tipping is not customary (see the money guide).

## At palaces and temples
Dress modestly at temples, speak quietly, and follow posted rules about photography. Some
temple halls are active places of worship, so be respectful of people praying. At palaces,
stay on designated paths and do not touch or climb on structures. Wearing hanbok (traditional
dress) often gives free or discounted palace entry, which is why you may see visitors in rented
hanbok; confirm current entry rules at the official palace information.

## Public behavior
Keep your voice down on public transit and give priority seats to elderly, pregnant, and
disabled passengers. Eating is generally avoided on the subway. Littering and jaywalking are
frowned upon. Smoking is only allowed in designated areas.
