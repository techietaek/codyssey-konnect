# Money and language basics

## Currency and payments
The currency is the Korean won (KRW, symbol ₩). Credit and debit cards are widely accepted,
including in convenience stores and for transit, but carry some cash for small vendors,
traditional markets, and street food. ATMs labeled "Global" or "International" accept foreign
cards; you will find them in convenience stores, banks, and subway stations.

## Tipping
Tipping is not customary in Korea and is not expected in restaurants, taxis, or hotels. Prices
are generally all-inclusive, and some restaurants add a service charge that is already shown on
the bill. You do not need to leave extra, and staff may even try to return a tip.

## Tax refund
Many stores offer tax-free shopping for tourists above a minimum purchase. Look for a "Tax Free"
sign, keep your receipts and passport, and claim the refund at the airport kiosks or in-store
where available. Rules and minimums change, so confirm details at the official tax-refund service.

## Useful Korean phrases
- Hello: 안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo)
- Thank you: 감사합니다 (gamsahamnida)
- Yes / No: 네 (ne) / 아니요 (aniyo)
- Excuse me / Sorry: 실례합니다 (sillyehamnida) / 죄송합니다 (joesonghamnida)
- How much is it?: 얼마예요? (eolmayeyo?)
- Where is the bathroom?: 화장실 어디예요? (hwajangsil eodiyeyo?)
- I don't speak Korean: 한국어 못해요 (hangugeo motae-yo)
English is spoken at major attractions, hotels, and transit, but less so with older people and in
small shops, so a translation app helps.

## Emergencies
For emergencies, 112 reaches the police and 119 reaches fire and ambulance services. The 1330
Korea Travel Hotline offers tourist help and interpretation in several languages, around the clock.
